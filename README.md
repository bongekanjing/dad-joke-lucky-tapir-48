## The joke

> I got a job at the library. My son asked what I do all day. I said, I check things out.

87 characters of a 200 character budget.

## Run it

```sh
python3 -P -s main.py
```

Writes `$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`, or `./entry.txt` when unset. The
destination directory is created if it is missing. Standard library only.

```sh
python3 -c "print(len(open('entry.txt',encoding='utf-8').read().strip()))"
```

## Why it groans

1. It is a joke a father tells at the table: a small boast about a dull job, a
   kid asking the obvious question, and a straight-faced answer. Nothing in it
   is trying to be clever at the kid's expense.
2. The pun lands on the first read. "Check things out" is what a librarian does
   and also what anyone does when they look something over, and the whole joke
   is those two meanings colliding inside three words. No one has to have it
   explained.
3. It is fresh in the telling rather than worn smooth. The library-worker pun is
   old, but the son's question reframes it as a small story instead of a riddle,
   which is what keeps it inside the tradition without reheating it.
4. It is tight. Three short sentences, each one setting up the last three words,
   and the punch arrives at the very end where a dad joke needs it.
5. It reads aloud. The rhythm is question and answer across a dinner table, and
   "I check things out" is the flat, pleased delivery of a father who knows he
   has got you.
